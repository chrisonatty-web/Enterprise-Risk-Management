## Project Overview

This project presents an Enterprise Risk Management (ERM) assessment for **PayNest Technologies**, a fictional growing Nigerian fintech company providing digital payment services through web and mobile platforms.

The organisation processes financial transactions, stores sensitive customer information, operates using cloud infrastructure, employs approximately 100 people, and depends on multiple third-party vendors.

As the business expands, its existing departmental approach to risk management becomes increasingly inadequate. The absence of an integrated enterprise-wide risk management framework creates challenges in identifying, assessing, monitoring, and managing interconnected business risks.

This project addresses these challenges by developing an enterprise-wide risk assessment and recommending appropriate risk management strategies using recognised frameworks and methodologies.

## Problem Statement

PayNest Technologies manages risks independently across different departments without a comprehensive enterprise-wide risk assessment.

This fragmented approach creates several challenges:

- Limited visibility of the organisation's overall risk exposure.
- Inconsistent risk identification and assessment.
- Unclear risk ownership and accountability.
- Increasing exposure to cybersecurity threats and data breaches.
- Potential operational disruptions arising from cloud infrastructure and third-party dependencies.
- Regulatory compliance and reputational risks.
- Difficulty making informed, risk-based business decisions.

The project aims to establish a structured ERM approach that provides management with a consolidated view of organisational risks and supports better decision-making.

## Project Objectives

The key objectives of this project are to:

1. Identify and classify PayNest's critical business assets.
2. Evaluate organisational risks using likelihood and impact assessments.
3. Establish risk appetite and risk tolerance across key business areas.
4. Apply recognised risk management frameworks to structure the ERM approach.
5. Conduct threat modelling to identify potential cybersecurity threats.
6. Establish risk ownership and accountability using the Three Lines Model.
7. Recommend appropriate risk treatment strategies.
8. Provide management with actionable recommendations to strengthen organisational resilience.

## Risk Management Frameworks

The assessment incorporates three recognised frameworks:

### 1. ISO 31000 – Risk Management

Used as a foundation for establishing a structured approach to identifying, analysing, evaluating, treating, monitoring, and communicating organisational risks.

### 2. COSO Enterprise Risk Management (ERM)

Used to support the integration of risk management with organisational strategy, performance, governance, and business objectives.

### 3. NIST Risk Management Framework (RMF)

Used to provide a structured approach to information security and cybersecurity risk management, particularly in relation to protecting sensitive information and technology assets.

## Key Project Activities and Deliverables

### 1. Business Context and Risk Assessment

Reviewed PayNest's business operations, organisational structure, technology environment, customer information, and third-party dependencies.

Identified the need for a coordinated enterprise-wide risk management approach.

### 2. Asset Identification and Classification

Developed an asset register identifying critical organisational assets, their owners, classifications, confidentiality, integrity, availability, and overall criticality.

Key assets assessed include:

- Customer databases.
- Laptops, servers, and mobile devices.
- Applications and cloud systems.
- People and business processes.
- Financial resources.
- Intellectual property.
- Physical facilities.
- Reputation and customer trust.
- Payment infrastructure.

The customer database and applications/cloud systems were classified as very high criticality assets.

### 3. Enterprise Risk Register

Developed an enterprise risk register using a likelihood and impact scoring methodology.

**Risk Scoring Formula:**

Risk Score = Likelihood × Impact

Both likelihood and impact were assessed using a five-point scale.

The assessment identified nine major organisational risks:

| Risk | Category | Score | Risk Level |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Cybersecurity | Technology | 20 | High |
| Cloud outage | Operational | 15 | Medium |
| Data breach | IT | 20 | High |
| Reputational damage | Strategic | 20 | High |
| Privacy | Compliance | 15 | Medium |
| Insider threat | Operational | 15 | Medium |
| Data manipulation | IT | 15 | Medium |
| Unauthorised disclosure | IT | 25 | High |
| Data loss | Operational | 25 | High |

### 4. Risk Appetite and Risk Tolerance

Established risk appetite and tolerance considerations across five business areas:

- **Cybersecurity:** Minimal risk appetite, with zero tolerance for unauthorised access to sensitive information.
- **Financial:** Moderate risk appetite to support growth, liquidity, and sustainable returns, with spending kept within approved budgets.
- **Operational:** Low appetite for operational failure and disruption of critical services.
- **Compliance:** Minimal appetite for breaches of applicable laws and regulations.
- **Third-party:** Low appetite for supplier-related risks, with no engagement with prohibited suppliers.

These considerations provide a basis for evaluating whether identified risks remain within acceptable organisational boundaries.

### 5. Cybersecurity Threat Modelling

Applied three threat-modelling approaches to examine potential threats to PayNest's customer information and technology environment.

**STRIDE**

Examined threats relating to:

- Spoofing / impersonation.
- Tampering.
- Repudiation.
- Information disclosure.
- Denial of service.
- Elevation of privilege.

**PASTA**

Considered the business objective of protecting customer data, the technical scope of data and storage, the system architecture, and potential cybercriminal threat actors.

**Attack Tree Analysis**

Explored potential attack paths involving:

- Unauthorised access to customer data stored in the cloud.
- Privilege escalation.
- Service disruption.

The threat-modelling exercise highlighted the potential consequences of exposed databases and cloud storage, including customer data exposure, service disruption, and regulatory consequences.

### 6. Three Lines Model

Applied the Three Lines Model to establish risk ownership, oversight, and independent assurance.

| Line | Responsible Functions | Responsibilities |
|---|---|---|
| First Line | IT, Head of Operations, Business Managers, Head of Procurement | Own and manage risks. |
| Second Line | Risk Managers, Compliance Teams, Information Security Officers | Support, monitor, and challenge risk management activities. |
| Third Line | Internal Auditors | Provide independent assurance. |

This model supports accountability and strengthens organisational risk governance.

### 7. Risk Treatment and Residual Risk Assessment

Developed a risk treatment register identifying risk owners, treatment approaches, and expected residual risk levels.

The primary treatment strategy was **risk mitigation/reduction** through the implementation of controls designed to reduce the likelihood or impact of identified risks.

The treatment register covered all nine identified risks.

The assessment assumed that risks initially identified as high could be reduced following control implementation, with low residual risk levels anticipated.

These residual risk ratings represent expected outcomes rather than independently verified control effectiveness.

### 8. Executive Risk Reporting and Recommendations

Prepared an executive summary highlighting the major threats to PayNest's business continuity, financial performance, regulatory standing, and stakeholder confidence.

Key recommendations included:

- Adopt a cybersecurity framework such as NIST to structure governance and control implementation.
- Implement strong encryption for sensitive information both at rest and in transit.
- Monitor customer feedback to understand stakeholder concerns.
- Review data protection and cybersecurity controls.
- Test crisis communication procedures.
- Strengthen management oversight of interconnected organisational risks.

## Limitations and Assumptions

The following limitations should be considered when interpreting the assessment:

- The project uses a fictional fintech organisation as its case study.
- Risk scores are based on a five-point likelihood and impact scale.
- Residual risk ratings are assumed expected outcomes following control implementation.
- Actual control testing and independent validation are not documented.
- Detailed implementation plans, control owners' action dates, and monitoring metrics are not included in the supplied documentation.
- Financial impact quantification and detailed third-party vendor assessments are not provided.
